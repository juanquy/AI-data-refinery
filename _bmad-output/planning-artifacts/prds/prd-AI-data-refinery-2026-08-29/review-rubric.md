# PRD Quality Review — Universal Data Refinery (prd-AI-data-refinery-2026-08-29)

Reviewed against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Inputs: `prd.md`, `addendum.md`. Stakes: launch-gating PRD for a two-founder proprietary product; brownfield (AI-generated prototype repo); chain-top (feeds UX, architecture, stories).

## Overall verdict

This is a decision-ready PRD with unusually honest bones: the launch persona is an explicit falsifiable hypothesis with a metric built to kill it, prototype fabrications are named requirement-by-requirement and retired, trade-offs are recorded with what was given up, and the counter-metrics section does real work. What's at risk is concentrated in two load-bearing definitions left open — what counts as a "query" in a business whose differentiator is billing, and what "security review passed" means in a launch whose entire premise is quality-gating — plus a very broad launch scope with no pre-agreed cut order, and human-surface journeys delegated (implicitly, not explicitly) to downstream UX. None of this breaks the PRD; all of it is where downstream work will stub its toe first.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions, with their supersessions on record: FR-062 "*Match-Apify decision: account-less payment is launch scope*," FR-081's 80/20 "*supersedes the prototype's 70/30*," FR-082 "*supersedes an earlier accrual-only plan*," and the addendum's "Rejected alternative: open-core / build-in-public" names exactly what was given up ("*source-visibility as a trust signal … the community feedback loop … open-core as a distribution channel*") and when to revisit. The Open Questions table (§7) is genuinely open — owners, revisit triggers, and one honestly struck-through resolution (OQ-7). §1 states the revenue posture against its own hype: agent billing is "*an option on the future, not a launch revenue expectation*." §2's "Named risk — the moat is the combination … Every competitor is one feature from parity" is the opposite of smoothed-to-neutral, and it's wired to a mechanism (the §6 standing market checkpoint with named trigger events). FR-061 handles the pricing tension cleanly: structure committed, dollars `[PLACEHOLDER]`, tracked as OQ-2.

Two places where the decision surface is thinner than the launch stakes want:

### Findings
- **high** Exit gate's decisive term is undefined (§6 Phase 0 exit gate) — "*Security review passed; zero known critical or high defects*" names no reviewer, standard, or scope, in a roadmap that explicitly says gates "*lean deliberately on independent verification … rather than on the builder's own assessment*." For a two-founder, AI-assisted build, a self-administered "security review passed" is exactly the builder's-own-assessment failure mode the section warns against — and this gate is the launch decision. *Fix:* name the mechanism (external reviewer, or a named tool-assisted audit + checklist) and what "passed" means (e.g., all findings ≥ high remediated and re-verified).
- **medium** No cut order for the scope-vs-speed decision (§3 launch scope vs. §6 checkpoint) — F1–F10 are all launch-gated, including marketplace with live Stripe Connect payouts, dual agent-payment modes, hybrid search, and fine-tuning export. The market checkpoint "*forces an explicit scope-vs-speed decision*" if a competitor moves, but the PRD gives that future decision no raw material: nothing ranks which launch commitments fall out first. *Fix:* add a one-paragraph de-scope priority (e.g., marketplace payouts → export → search sophistication survive/fall in stated order) so the checkpoint firing produces a decision, not a re-litigation.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona theater: one launch persona, explicitly tagged "*[ASSUMPTION — validate post-launch]*" (§2), and it drives real requirements — the free tier ("*'playing' cannot sit behind a paywall*"), FR-111's MCP config generation for "*the agentic IDEs the launch persona lives in*," FR-013's choice of which vertical is genuinely live, and the §5 MCP-connection-rate metric that "*exists to test the hypothesis*." No NFR theater: the NFR section's signature move is refusing unearned numbers — "*no public latency claim until telemetry exists*" (NFR-001), "*99.998% uptime constant is retired*" (NFR-010), "*Fabricated metrics are a defect*" (FR-072). Differentiation is researched, not asserted: §1 names Context7 as the nearest occupant and concedes "*differentiation there rests entirely on the validated, versioned, severity-rated half*"; FR-012 volunteers that SEC 10-K "*is the most contested of the six … so it is not marketing-forward*." The Vision could not swap into another PRD. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated and bet on: a validated, versioned, severity-rated corpus that both humans and agents can query *and pay for* natively — "*the moat is the combination*" (§2). Features trace to it: F1 validation, F2/F3 the versioned-corpus-with-change-intelligence half, F4/F7 the agent-native-access-and-billing half; even F10's export earns its place via "*diff-derived training examples … a distinct dataset the change intelligence uniquely enables*" (FR-090). Success metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity — bailout count is explicitly "*the counterweight to signup vanity*," and counter-metrics (cost-per-query vs. price, hallucination reports, takedown requests) are present and product-specific, which most PRDs never manage. Scope kind is a coherent platform launch whose logic matches the M2M bet.

### Findings
- **medium** Marketplace has no supply side (F9, §5, §6) — F9 commits to live creator payouts at launch ("*Creators are paid live from launch: Stripe Connect onboarding, tax handling, and transfers*"), but nothing anywhere says where launch creators come from, and §5 carries no marketplace metric (no listings count, no creator activations). A two-sided surface with launch-scope payment plumbing and zero demand/supply hypothesis is the least thesis-driven commitment in the PRD. *Fix:* either state the seeding plan (founders publish the six templates as the initial listings, creators recruited in Phase 2) and add one creator-side metric to §5, or move live payouts to a fast-follow.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The FR hygiene is well above typical: the per-FR prototype annotations function as negative acceptance criteria ("*Prototype gap: `/mcp` bypasses all metering*" FR-032; "*confidence scores are hardcoded 0.96–0.99 literals*" FR-006; "*top-up grants quota without charging*" FR-063 — each names the exact defect whose absence is "done"). FR-022's webhook contract (filters honored, fan-out to all matches, signed, retried, test-event affordance) and the §6 exit gate's concrete conditions ("*14 consecutive days with no manual intervention*") are engineer-ready. But the rubric says be unforgiving here, and there are holes on load-bearing items:

### Findings
- **high** "Query" — the unit the business bills in — is never defined (FR-060/061/062/065, §5) — quotas ("*10k queries*"), agent pricing ("*$0.005*" per query), and the ledger ("*every billable query*") all denominate in a unit the PRD never bounds. Does a cached corpus read bill the same as an on-demand refinement? Do search, version-history, and diff reads count against quota? NFR-050 distinguishes "*cached reads and extractions*" in the *cost* model but nothing distinguishes them in the *billing* model. Every pricing decision (OQ-2, OQ-3) and FR-065's reconciliation inherit this ambiguity. *Fix:* define "billable query" once (and whether read classes are metered differently), and use that term in F7 and §5.
- **medium** FR-052 names the role matrix but doesn't give it — "*the four-role matrix OWNER / BUILDER / MEMBER / VIEWER with per-capability rights (schema design, refinement, invites, billing, audit-log visibility)*" supplies both axes and no cells. An engineer cannot know whether MEMBER can design schemas or BUILDER can see the audit log; story creation will have to invent the answer. *Fix:* include the 4×5 matrix table (it fits in six lines) or cite the authoritative source for it.
- **medium** FR-004 "degrade gracefully" (F1) — the rubric's flagged phrase verbatim: "*model failures degrade gracefully and are visible in provenance*." Fallback ordering, retry semantics, and what the caller observes on double failure are all unstated. *Fix:* state the testable consequence — e.g., primary failure triggers exactly one fallback attempt; provenance records which model served; both failing returns an explicit error, never a partial extraction.
- **low** NFR-001 has no internal bound — "*target low-latency edge serving*" is an adjective. Withholding *public* claims until measured is right and admirable, but engineers building the cache tier still need a budget. *Fix:* set an internal, non-published target (e.g., cached-read p95 < Xms at edge) labeled as such.
- **low** FR-064 "immediate kill-switch" is unbounded — immediate on the next request, or within N seconds across edge caches and in-flight sessions? For the guardian trust story this bound is the feature. *Fix:* state the revocation-propagation bound.
- **low** NFR-051 "launch scale" is unquantified — "*scale horizontally without customer-visible capacity planning at launch scale*" defers the only number that makes it testable. *Fix:* state the assumed launch load envelope (even order-of-magnitude: queries/day, tracked entities).

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit at every altitude: §1 Scope Commitments rules out "*regulated-enterprise sales requiring BAA/SOC 2 procurement*" at launch; §3 has an "Explicitly Out of Scope" section that removes the promotions module by name; NFR-032/NFR-035 state known non-offerings "*plainly wherever enterprise buyers would look*"; Phase 4 is titled "*conditional, not promised*." De-scoping happened in the open — FR-100/101 post-launch with prototype status noted, FR-013 restricting "genuinely live" to one vertical with the other five as "*clearly labeled sample data, never presented as live coverage*." Both `[ASSUMPTION]` tags sit on the two inferences that matter most (launch persona, path-to-paid), each with a validation plan. Open-items density is right for the stakes: eight OQs, all parameter-level (name, dollars, quota size, source list) rather than direction-level — none blocks build, and each has a revisit trigger. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` convention is absent, but its function is served by the OQ table and italic tension notes; no information is lost.

### Findings
- **low** §1's "Five product surfaces" list overstates launch surface read alone — it marks SDKs "*(TypeScript, LangChain, LlamaIndex — launch)*" but leaves "browser extension, and a CLI" unmarked; only F11 reveals both are post-launch. A reader of §1 alone assumes five surfaces at launch. *Fix:* append "(post-launch)" to the extension and CLI in the §1 list.

## Downstream usability — adequate

Cross-references are in good order: every FR/NFR/OQ reference in §4–§7 resolves (FR-122→FR-113, FR-112→FR-005/NFR-020, Phase 3→OQ-8), IDs are unique with deliberate block gaps, and sections extract cleanly — F7 or F3 pulled alone carries its own context. FR-021 even does glossary-style work inline, mandating the two severity vocabularies be "*defined once and used consistently across API, MCP, badges, and UI*." The addendum is a model of chain-top practice: architecture pointers (402 headers, embedding recipe, KV cache) parked where they belong instead of bloating FRs. But the PRD feeds UX → architecture → stories, and two structural aids are missing:

### Findings
- **medium** No Glossary (whole document) — domain nouns (workspace, tracked entity, guardian, agent token, wallet, blueprint, template pack, courtesy action, corpus, refinement) are used *mostly* consistently, but the one place terms do drift — "refined queries" (§5) vs. "billable query" (FR-065) vs. bare "queries" (FR-061) — is precisely the high-severity ambiguity flagged under Done-ness. A glossary would have caught it. For a chain-top PRD this is the cheapest insurance in the document. *Fix:* add a ~12-term glossary; make "billable query" its anchor entry.

## Shape fit — strong

The brownfield handling is exemplary — the best thing in the PRD. "*Where this PRD and the prototype disagree, the PRD is authoritative*" is stated up front and §3 restates it, and every FR carries a specific, checkable prototype status ("*three hardcoded passcodes*" FR-070; "*the cron lists pipelines but never executes them*" FR-011; "*audit tables read but never written*" FR-073). New-vs-existing is never ambiguous. The capability-spec shape fits the machine-facing core (MCP/REST/agents) exactly right — UJs for autonomous agents would be theater. One shape miss:

### Findings
- **medium** Zero user journeys for the human surfaces (F5, F7 guardian flow, F8, F9) — the product has at least five distinct human actors: the launch-persona developer, the guardian ("*funds the wallet and holds a kill-switch*"), the marketplace creator (lists, prices, gets paid), the workspace admin, and the founder-operator (F8). Studio is a no-code visual builder — meaningful UX by any measure — and its create→test→publish→provision flow exists only implied across FR-040–043. The downstream UX phase will have to invent every journey from FR fragments. *Fix:* either add three short UJs with named protagonists (persona activation end-to-end; guardian funds-and-kills; creator lists-and-gets-paid), or state explicitly that journey design is delegated to the UX phase — as a decision, not an omission.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID ordering anomalies** (harmless, but position-sensitive extraction will misorder them): F9 lists FR-082 after FR-083/084; feature sections run F10 → **F13 → F12** → F11 (F13 before F12 looks like a late-edit accident — the launch/post-launch grouping explains F11 last but not F13/F12 inversion); NFR-013 sits between NFR-011 and NFR-012.
- **Quota-vs-dollar commitment nuance in FR-061**: "*Dollar amounts are [PLACEHOLDER]*" — leaving the 10k/100k quotas apparently committed while OQ-3 covers only the free-tier quota. If paid quotas are also pending the Phase 2 pricing exercise, say so.
- **Title drift**: frontmatter/H1 say "AI Data Refinery" / "Universal Data Refinery"; the preamble's working-name note covers this. OQ-1 will resolve it.
- **Glossary drift**: none beyond the "query" family already flagged; "tracked entity," "workspace," "guardian" are used identically throughout.
- Cross-references: all resolve; no dangling IDs; OQ-7 correctly struck through with its resolution pointer (FR-082).
- Required sections for the agreed stakes: Vision, customers/positioning, FRs, NFRs, Success Metrics, roadmap, Open Questions all present; Glossary and Assumptions Index are the two absences (findings above).
